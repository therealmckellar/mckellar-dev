Moving from an MCA brokerage model to a direct funding platform is one of the biggest structural leaps in alternative business finance. Brokerages scale with relatively low balance-sheet risk and lean overhead, earning commissions by placing paper with external funders. Becoming a direct funder unlocks stronger long-term margins, control over your own credit box, recurring renewal economics, and lasting portfolio enterprise value—but it also exposes you directly to capital allocation, underwriting discipline, servicing, and default risk.

Having helped build and scale direct lending infrastructure as Vice President and Executive Board Member at Whetstone Holdings (DBA Everest Funding, Vader Funding, and Granite Mountain Funding) and now leading revenue strategy as CRO at My Commercial Funding, I have worked closely on both sides of the table. Here are the foundational pieces that separate durable direct funders from shops that overextend in their first six months. Read more about my background on [mckellar.dev/richard-mckellar](/richard-mckellar) and [ventures](/ventures).

## 1. Establish Clean Risk Grading Before Chasing Volume

New direct funders often get tempted to buy volume by loosening underwriting rules on tough files. That buys short-term funding numbers and long-term portfolio headaches. Before deploying substantial capital, build an explicit risk-grading framework tied to real underwriting fundamentals:

- Average daily ledger balances and revenue stability across multiple months
- NSF counts, negative-balance days, and deposit consistency (filtering out internal transfers or one-off spikes)
- Existing position stacking, debt-service coverage, and industry-specific seasonality
- Verified business tenure and owner background checks

Back-testing those risk grades against real payment performance lets you price factor rates, terms, and advance caps fairly while protecting capital partners.

## 2. Design Syndication Mechanics Early

Very few direct funders begin purely on internal equity. Most grow safely by partnering with experienced syndication participants alongside structured debt or warehouse facilities. Attracting reliable syndication partners comes down to **transparency and reporting hygiene**:

- Clear deal summaries showing bank metrics, position stack, and approval rationale
- Exact pro-rata participation tracking from initial wire through daily/weekly ACH remittances
- Clean exception reporting when an account pauses, modifies, or enters collections

When your reporting earns trust with early syndicate partners, expanding your capital capacity becomes dramatically smoother as ISO submissions grow.

## 3. Connect Your CRM, Underwriting, and Servicing Ledger

Nothing slows down a growing direct funder faster than disconnected back-office systems. When ISO intake lives in email, underwriting happens in spreadsheets, contracts live in DocuSign folders, and servicing data sits in a separate ACH platform, reconciliation turns into a full-time bottleneck.

Integrating your [CRM architecture](/blog/crm-setup-buildout), underwriting checklist, contract generation, and portfolio ledger creates immediate operational leverage. Reps see real-time deal statuses, underwriters get complete packets without chasing missing stips, and management can evaluate ISO performance by **net portfolio performance**—not just gross funded dollars.

## 4. Use AI Agents to Multiply Underwriter Throughput Without Losing Control

Today's direct funders have a major advantage over shops built ten years ago: you no longer need a massive back-office headcount just to pre-screen incoming bank statements and broker packets. Deploying practical [AI underwriting triage](/blog/ai-underwriting) and automated ISO notifications lets experienced underwriters focus strictly on credit decisions and merchant calls rather than manual PDF transcription.
